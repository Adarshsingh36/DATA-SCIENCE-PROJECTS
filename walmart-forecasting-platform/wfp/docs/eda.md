# Exploratory data analysis (descriptive; nothing here feeds model features)

Generated by `python -m walmartfc.pipeline eda` -> `reports/results/eda_summary.json`, figures in `reports/figures/`.

* **Scale and gaps.** 3,331 store-department series over 143 weeks; 2,660 have a complete history. 11.5% of the series x week grid is unobserved
  (late starts, early ends, gaps). These cells are kept as missing (not zero) for features and scoring.
* **Concentration.** Top 5 departments carry 28.5% of sales, top 10 stores 39.1%; store Type A 64.3%, B 29.7%, C 6.0%.
* **Holidays are not uniform.** Versus the previous four-week average, total sales rise about +15% in the Super Bowl week and +43% in the Thanksgiving week,
  are flat at Labor Day, and are **-29% in Kaggle's "Christmas" holiday week** (week ending Dec 30/31, *after* Christmas). The real peak is the week
  *before* Christmas (+12% vs the prior four weeks on average, far more in many departments). `IsHoliday` alone would mislead a model; hence week-distance-to-Christmas/Thanksgiving features.
* **Spikes were investigated, not removed.** Using a robust z-score > 5 against each series' median/MAD, 9,204 observations qualify; 49.0% fall in
  holiday/Thanksgiving/pre-Christmas weeks that are only 11.2% of all weeks (4.4x over-representation). The rest are department-specific
  events and clearance/seasonal lines. They are genuine demand and stay in the data.
* **Negative sales:** 1,285 rows (0.30%, -$88k in total) are returns/adjustments; kept.
* **Exogenous variables.** Raw pooled correlations with store totals are weak and confounded by season (temperature -0.11, fuel -0.01, CPI +0.06,
  unemployment -0.06, markdown total +0.14); they are given to the model as context, not relied on. Markdowns exist only from Nov 2011.
